# Game Design Document: KILL THE BILL

## Setting
A near-future dystopia where wealth inequality has reached its logical extreme. The world is divided between **The Enclave** (pristine corporate plazas where the top 0.1% live) and **The Sinks** (brutalist slums suffocating under poverty, pollution, and Enforcer patrols). A handful of Oligarchs control food, technology, security, and media — the four pillars of survival.

## Narrative Pillars
1. **Class Warfare as Gameplay:** Every system reflects the divide. Prices, NPC moods, security presence, and political alignment are all variables the player can manipulate.
2. **No Good Options:** The player is forced to make morally complex choices. Assassinating an Oligarch causes a power vacuum. Starting a revolution gets people killed. Economic hacking starves the poor before it bankrupts the rich.
3. **Emergent Storytelling:** No scripted narrative. The story emerges from the intersection of systemic variables, LLM-generated events, and the player's choices. Two playthroughs will never tell the same story.
4. **People, Not Pawns:** The 40 persistent NPCs are not quest-givers. They are individuals with personalities, quirks, fears, and memories. The player can form real relationships (friendship, romance, operational trust) or exploit them as disposable assets.

## Core Gameplay Loop
1. **Observe:** Read the NetFeed. Check Oligarch public image. Talk to NPCs. Assess the Sector Zeitgeist.
2. **Plan:** Identify targets, vulnerabilities, and allies. Choose an approach: stealth, persuasion, sabotage, or violence.
3. **Act:** Execute the plan. Actions ripple through the Butterfly Effect. The economy shifts. NPCs react. The NetFeed reports (or hides) the consequences.
4. **Adapt:** The world has changed. New opportunities and threats emerge. Allies may have radicalized, broken, or been arrested. The player adapts and loops back to Observe.

## Player Archetypes
- **White Collar (The Disgraced Elite):** High Intel (passwords, map data, security overrides). Low Social Capital (hated by the masses). Crisis: Hunted by the Compliance AI.
- **Blue Collar (The Discarded Laborer):** Low Intel. High Social Capital (black market access, NPC allies). Crisis: Resource Squeeze (constant survival costs).

## Player Characteristics
At the start of a run, the player allocates "Background Points" to traits that act as global modifiers:
- **Corporate Face:** Charisma. Bribes cost 50% less. White Collar NPCs start friendlier.
- **Ghost in the Machine:** Tech savvy. Hacking is more effective but less stealthy.
- **Street Agitator:** Populism. Political pressure is 2x effective. Entering a sector raises tension by +10.

## Regions
1. **The Mega-Cities:** Dense urban centers split between The Sinks (brutalist slums) and The Enclave (corporate plazas).
2. **The Countryside:** Subterranean farms, strip mines, automated factories. Low population, critical for economic sabotage.
3. **The Islands:** Heavily guarded Oligarch retreats. Maximum security, minimum witnesses.

## Roguelite Persistence
Death is not the end. The player spawns as a new citizen with a new Class Seed. But the WorldDirector state persists: if your last character triggered a food shortage, your new character must survive the consequences. The world remembers even when the player doesn't.

## Target Experience
The player should feel like a revolutionary operating inside a living, breathing system that reacts to everything they do. The world is not waiting for the player — it's evolving on its own through LLM-generated events, Oligarch decisions, and NPC psychology. The player's job is to read the system, find the cracks, and exploit them before the system crushes them.
