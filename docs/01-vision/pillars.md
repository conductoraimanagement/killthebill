# Design Pillars

Five principles. Every mechanic, UI choice, and system design answers to these. When a decision is ambiguous, fall back here.

---

## 1. Class warfare as gameplay
The economy, the NPCs' moods, the security on the streets, the alignment of the Senate — these are not flavor. They are **variables the player manipulates**. The game's fiction and mechanics are the same object.

**Applies to:** every screen shows one of these variables. Every action changes one. If a feature doesn't move a class-warfare needle, cut it.

---

## 2. No good options
There is no "right" path. Assassinating an Oligarch creates a power vacuum that often makes things worse. Starving the economy starves the poor first. Inciting a revolution gets people killed. The player should finish a run with the satisfaction of a job done — and the weight of what it cost.

**Applies to:** victory endings must have costs. NPC reactions must acknowledge them. No clean wins.

---

## 3. Emergent storytelling
No scripted narrative. Stories come from the intersection of systemic variables, LLM-generated events, the billionaires' autonomous actions, NPC psychology, cameo encounters, and the player's choices. Two playthroughs should never tell the same story.

**Applies to:** do not write dialogue trees. Do not script missions. The LLM is the narrator; the systems are the plot.

---

## 4. People, not pawns
The 40 persistent NPCs are not quest-givers. They are individuals — name, personality, quirks, fears, memory. The player can love them, betray them, radicalize them, or use them. The Oligarchs are the same: not health bars but characters with paranoia arcs, vanity spirals, and ambitions that don't care about the player.

**Applies to:** no respawns. No generic crowds. Every named character has a personality seed and memory.

---

## 5. The world does not wait
The simulation evolves every cycle whether the player acts or not. Oligarchs pursue their ambitions. NPCs radicalize or break. The economy tilts. The NetFeed reports. A run where the player "does nothing" is not frozen — it's just a run where the system crushes them without their help.

**Applies to:** every idle tick should have movement. If the player spends a day watching the NetFeed, something should have changed by the end of it.
