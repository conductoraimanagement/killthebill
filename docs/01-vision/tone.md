# Tone

## The world
A near-future dystopia where wealth inequality has reached its logical extreme. Four pillars of survival — **food, tech, security, media** — each owned by a single billionaire. A fifth and sixth — **pharma, energy** — sometimes join them, sometimes don't. The Senate is theater; the real parliament is a handful of boardrooms.

Two poles define the map:
- **The Enclave** — pristine, symmetrical, bright. Clean air. Sunlight. A surveillance state that looks like a garden.
- **The Sinks** — brutalist, vertical, dense. Neon rain. Patrol drones. The smell of frying wiring. The only place where people still talk to each other.

Between them: countryside of subterranean farms and strip mines. Checkpoints that turn borders into chokepoints. Island retreats where the real money lives, behind comm jammers and private militias.

## The feel
- **Immersive sim, not action game.** Observation first, violence last. The player should feel like they're reading a city, not mowing through waves.
- **Paranoid and dense.** The NetFeed ticker is always running. Something is always happening somewhere. The world is noisier than the player.
- **Systemic, not theatrical.** Consequences are arithmetic, not cutscenes. A food riot is +15 tension and a price spike — the drama is what that number does to the people you know.
- **Absurd where it helps.** The billionaires are pathetic as much as they are terrifying. The NetFeed deadpans. A cultural cameo can drop a Tyler-Durden-shaped wrecking ball into a careful run and the game treats it with the same straight face it uses for famine.

## Reference palette
- **Disco Elysium** — moral ambiguity, systemic dialogue, a city as character
- **Fight Club / Mr. Robot** — anti-capitalist chaos aesthetic, distrust of the system
- **Deus Ex / Dishonored** — immersive sim signage, diegetic UI, multiple solutions
- **Hunger Games / Elysium / Blade Runner** — class-divided dystopia, the visual vocabulary
- **Network / Succession / Billions** — the billionaires themselves as pettily human monsters
- **The Wire** — systemic storytelling over heroes

## What this is not
- Not a power fantasy. The player is outgunned by design.
- Not a lecture. The politics are in the mechanics, not the script.
- Not grimdark. There is humor — black, sharp, frequent — especially in the NetFeed and cameos.
- Not a linear narrative. There is no "true ending". Each path costs something different.
